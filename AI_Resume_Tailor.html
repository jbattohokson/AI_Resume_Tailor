Executive Summary

Free local models are good at rewording and bad at not making things up. Instead of asking a second AI to review the first one, this tool wraps a CrewAI crew (job description analyst, resume editor, cover letter writer) in plain Python checks. A draft that invents a number, adds a skill you can't defend, slips in a banned word or misses the cover letter's 7-to-9-sentence shape goes back to the model with the exact problems listed. After two failed retries it's saved marked FLAGGED instead of passing quietly.

I ran it five times with qwen2.5 (7B) on three real postings. All three resumes passed on the first attempt. Both cover letters were flagged after three attempts, one for inventing a "50%" improvement. Those runs also exposed three gaps in my own checks, which I fixed and covered with tests.

Why code, not another agent

A second model reviewing the first shares its blind spots. A set difference between the numbers in the draft and the numbers in your sources doesn't.

Still not caught

Change logs written in another format, generic openings like "I am excited to apply", and letters that never name the company. These are listed in the README's limits, not hidden.

03A 7B model edits very conservatively

Across three resumes it made one real keyword edit. That's safe (nothing was fabricated), but it means the tool adds less value on a small model than on a stronger one. Switching to Claude is one line in .env; the checks don't change, so a stronger model just fails them less often.

Design

How it works

Resume mode:   JD Analyst ──> Resume Editor ──> guards ──┬─ pass ──> output/<job>_resume.txt
                                                         └─ fail ──> retry with the exact problems (max 2), then FLAGGED
Cover mode:    Hook Finder ──> Letter Writer ──> guards ──> output/<job>_cover.txt
CheckWhat it catches
New numbersAny figure not in the resume (or, for letters, the job description and notes)
Blocked claimsSkills on your "can't defend this" list that the draft adds
New namesTools or companies mid-sentence that appear in neither the resume nor the posting
Change logLogged edits missing from the resume
StyleEm dashes, banned words and their variants, placeholders, code fences
Letter shape7 to 9 sentences, no "I am writing...", no exclamation points
